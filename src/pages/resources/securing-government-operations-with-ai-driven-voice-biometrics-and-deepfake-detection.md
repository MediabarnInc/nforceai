As cyber threats evolve, safeguarding identity and communication in the public sector has become a top priority. With a surge in deepfake audio and voice cloning, traditional security measures are no longer enough. Federal agencies are turning to AI-driven voice biometrics to protect against fraud, unauthorized access and identity theft.

## Why voice biometrics?

Voice biometrics authenticate people in real time by analyzing their unique vocal characteristics. Unlike passwords or PINs, they offer a more reliable, personal form of security. The technology confirms identity with high mathematical precision and creates an irrevocable link between the person and the transaction, making it hard for fraudsters to fake or mimic.

## Combating deepfake threats

Deepfake audio lets malicious actors replicate a person’s voice, a significant threat to government agencies, especially in sensitive areas like defense and homeland security. nForce’s next-generation deepfake detection adds an essential layer of security, helping government entities detect and prevent these attacks in real time.

## Key benefits for government agencies

- **Stronger security:** voice biometrics create an authentication process that is very difficult to replicate or breach.
- **Fraud prevention:** real-time deepfake detection stops attempts to use voice cloning before they compromise sensitive data.
- **Operational efficiency:** automated voice authentication speeds up access to systems for staff and citizens alike.
- **Privacy by design:** the technology prioritizes user privacy and supports compliance with data protection regulations.

## Real-time identity assurance

By combining voice biometrics with advanced audio forensics, agencies can protect communications, transactions and operations. Verifying identities in real time and keeping an immutable record of transactions ensures non-repudiation, which is essential for regulatory compliance and auditability.
